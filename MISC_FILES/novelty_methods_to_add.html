Frontier: model architecture upgrades
Replace NN1
Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)
Purpose-built for multi-horizon time series with mixed-frequency inputs. Variable selection network automatically weights which features matter. Interpretable attention across time steps. Google Brain paper showed 36% improvement over LSTM on financial data.
Impact: +15–25% prediction accuracy on NN1
Ensemble NN1
N-BEATS / N-HiTS architecture
Basis expansion approach — decomposes price series into interpretable trend and seasonality components automatically. No explicit feature engineering. N-HiTS (2022) extends this to hierarchical multi-scale sampling, dramatically reducing inference cost on long sequences.
Impact: interpretable price decomposition
New NN6
Graph Neural Network (GNN) for correlations
Model the stock market as a dynamic graph: nodes = stocks, edges = rolling correlations + sector membership. GNN propagates information along edges — if AAPL moves, MSFT node updates. Captures contagion effects that independent models miss entirely.
Impact: cross-asset signal propagation
Data aug
FinGAN — synthetic data generation
Train a Wasserstein GAN conditioned on market regime to generate realistic synthetic tick sequences. Use generated data to augment rare market conditions (flash crashes, short squeezes) which have too few real samples for training. Dramatically improves tail-risk behavior of NN4.
Impact: better rare-event risk modeling
Frontier: uncertainty and risk
Reliability
Conformal Prediction intervals
Distribution-free method giving statistically guaranteed prediction intervals — "price will be in [X, Y] with 95% coverage" with a mathematical guarantee, not just empirical confidence. Works on top of any model. Unlike MC Dropout, requires no Bayesian assumptions. 2023 research shows strong results on equities.
Impact: legally defensible uncertainty bounds
Uncertainty
Deep kernel Gaussian Processes (DKL)
A deep neural network extracts features; a Gaussian Process on top provides principled uncertainty. Unlike MC Dropout, GP uncertainty grows in unexplored regions of the feature space — much better calibrated for regime shifts the model hasn't seen before.
Impact: superior uncertainty during regime shifts
Risk
CVaR / Expected Shortfall optimization
Replace VaR with CVaR (average loss in the worst X% of scenarios) as the optimization target. CVaR is convex — optimizable via linear programming. Coherent risk measure unlike VaR. Dramatically better tail-risk behavior, especially in fat-tailed financial distributions.
Impact: 30-50% reduction in tail-risk exposure
RL upgrade
Distributional RL (QR-DQN / IQN)
Instead of predicting expected return, predict the full return distribution. Implicit Quantile Networks (IQN) can then optimize for any quantile — e.g., "maximize 10th percentile return" for risk-averse investors. Fundamentally different from standard PPO's expected-value objective.
Impact: investor risk-tolerance encoded into RL objective
Frontier: training methodology
Training
MAML — meta-learning for new markets
Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning trains the model to be quickly fine-tunable. When your system encounters a new stock or market (e.g., adding an Indian mid-cap), MAML can adapt NN1 in 5–10 gradient steps rather than retraining from scratch — critical for scaling to hundreds of symbols.
Impact: fast adaptation to new assets
Training
Online continual learning (EWC / PackNet)
Markets shift regime. Standard NNs forget old patterns when retrained on new data (catastrophic forgetting). Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) adds a Fisher-information regularizer that protects important weights from old tasks — models can learn from new data without forgetting bear-market behavior.
Impact: no retraining from scratch on regime change
Training
Contrastive self-supervised pre-training
Pre-train NN1's encoder on massive unlabeled tick data using contrastive objectives (similar market conditions should have nearby embeddings; different conditions far apart). Like ImageNet pre-training for vision — gives NN1 a far better feature representation before any supervised fine-tuning.
Impact: +20% data efficiency in fine-tuning
Training
Curriculum learning for NN5 RL agent
Train NN5 on easy market scenarios first (bull market, low volatility), progressively introducing harder ones (flash crashes, earnings gaps, black swan events). Prevents the RL agent from learning degenerate strategies on hard scenarios early. Research shows 40% faster convergence.
Impact: more stable PPO training convergence
Frontier: inference and execution
Execution
FPGA NN inference deployment
Quantize NN1 to INT8 and deploy on an FPGA (e.g., Xilinx Alveo). FPGA inference latency: 100–500 nanoseconds vs 5–10 milliseconds on GPU. At true HFT scale (co-located with exchange), this latency difference determines whether you capture the alpha signal before competitors. Rust handles FPGA communication via PCIe.
Impact: 20–100× inference latency reduction
Inference
TensorRT + INT8 model quantization
Export all 5 PyTorch models to ONNX, then compile with NVIDIA TensorRT + INT8 post-training quantization. Typical result: 4–8× inference speedup with <0.5% accuracy loss. Critical when NN inference must complete before the next tick arrives (100ms windows at liquid markets).
Impact: 4–8× faster batch inference
Execution
Almgren-Chriss optimal execution in Rust
Once NN5 decides to buy X shares of AAPL, the execution engine must minimize market impact by slicing the order optimally over time. Almgren-Chriss model (implemented in Rust, naturally) gives the closed-form optimal trajectory balancing execution risk vs market impact cost.
Impact: 10–30% reduction in slippage cost
Trust
SHAP explainability layer
Add SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) to every NN decision. Each BUY/SELL recommendation shows the investor exactly which features drove the decision: "RSI oversold contributed 34%, negative earnings sentiment contributed 28%, regime is bullish contributed 21%." Builds investor trust and satisfies regulatory explainability requirements.
Impact: investor trust + regulatory compliance
