Backtesting traps — where most people declare false victory
kills 60% of projects
Look-ahead bias in backtesting
Using data that wasn't available at the time of the trade decision. The most seductive trap: your model sees tomorrow's open price while deciding today. Even subtle forms — using a daily close to trigger an intraday trade — produce fantasy backtest profits that vanish live.
Fix: strict point-in-time datasets, walk-forward validation only, never use adjusted prices for signals
kills 40% of strategies
Survivorship bias in training data
Training on today's S&P 500 constituents means your model never sees Lehman Brothers, Enron, or Kodak — the companies that failed and were removed from the index. Your model learns "survivors are safe" and dramatically underestimates downside risk on real portfolios.
Fix: use CRSP or Compustat point-in-time universe data, explicitly include delisted tickers
ignored by 80%
Ignoring realistic transaction costs
A strategy with 0.1% edge per trade that trades 50 times/day and ignores 0.05% bid-ask spread + 0.02% commission + 0.03% slippage actually loses money. Most retail backtests use mid-price fills, which never happen in live markets. HFT strategies die here first.
Fix: model fills at ask (buys) and bid (sells), add realistic slippage as function of order size / ADV
destroys 70% of NN strategies
Model overfitting to historical regimes
Neural networks trained on 2010–2020 bull market data learn "dips always recover." They have zero concept of prolonged bear markets because they've never seen one proportionally in training. The model confuses "what happened in this dataset" with "what markets do." NN5 is especially vulnerable.
Fix: train across multiple full market cycles, include 2001, 2008, 2020 explicitly, use regime-stratified validation
Live deployment failures — where paper-profit systems die in production
erodes 90% of edge within months
Alpha decay — signals expire faster than you retrain
Every profitable signal gets arbitraged away. Academic papers show ML-based price signals in liquid markets decay to noise within 3–12 months after discovery. Most builders deploy and forget. By the time you notice PnL degrading, the model is already blind. This is the single biggest long-term killer.
Fix: weekly model performance audits, automated regime-triggered retraining pipeline, decay detection alerts
ignored until AUM grows
Market impact and self-fulfillment at scale
At small size you're a price taker. At larger AUM, your own orders move the market. Worse: if enough traders use similar ML signals (which they do — FinBERT is public), correlated signals cause crowded trades and flash crashes driven by everyone exiting simultaneously. Your model's signal IS the crash.
Fix: position size caps as % of ADV, decorrelation from known public signals, proprietary alternative data
catastrophic loss during black swans
Regime shift blindness at critical moments
NN3 detects 4 regime states — but a black swan (COVID crash, SVB collapse, Flash Crash 2010) is state 5 that the model has never seen. The regime detector confidently outputs "sideways/low volatility" for 2–3 minutes while the market is already down 8%. NN5 keeps allocating normally into a waterfall.
Fix: hard Rust kill switch on VIX spike above 3σ, VWAP deviation circuit breaker, correlated asset stress monitor
overlooked until it is too late
Regulatory compliance — SEBI, SEC, MiFID II
In India, running algorithmic trading for others requires SEBI algo trading registration. In the US, managing others' money requires RIA registration. In Europe, MiFID II governs algorithmic systems. Operating without compliance exposes you to criminal liability. This shuts down more promising systems than technical failure does.
Fix: begin regulatory consultation at MVP stage, not after launch. Costs ₹2–5L in India, much more later
Data and infrastructure failures — silent killers
ignored by 90% of builders
Garbage-in data quality — silent P&L drain
Free and low-cost data APIs (Alpha Vantage, Yahoo Finance) have documented issues: missing ticks, duplicate rows, incorrect corporate actions, stale quotes during low-liquidity periods. A single corrupted tick that spikes RSI to 100 will make NN1 issue a violent sell signal on perfectly normal data. Nobody checks data quality post-deployment.
Fix: Rust-side data validation layer — outlier detection, timestamp gap alerts, cross-source consensus checks
silent failure — nobody notices
Silent WebSocket gaps causing stale features
A WebSocket connection drops for 4 seconds during a volatile period. Rust reconnects. But the order book and feature window now have a 4-second gap in the middle. RSI computed on this gapped series is mathematically wrong. The model doesn't know the data is stale — it confidently acts on corrupted features. Happens multiple times per day.
Fix: Rust timestamp gap detector — mark features as STALE if last tick older than threshold, block order submission until fresh
